Critical minerals, by the numbers

Market size, prices, demand growth, supply concentration, and government funding for lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite, and rare earths, from published sources.

Two different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the market: demand growth, prices, and market value, counted by the International Energy Agency and the price reporters each month. The second kind measures the politics of supply: who mines, who refines, and how much public money is now being committed to change the answer. The first kind tells a reader how big the business is. The second tells a reader who controls it.

What the latest numbers show is a market pulling in two directions at once: demand keeps rising — lithium demand grew about 30% in 2023 and by nearly 30% again in 2024 — while prices fell hard from their peaks and investment in new supply stalled. Governments filled part of the gap: public-finance commitments in advanced economies reached about $65 billion in 2025, more than four times the 2023 level, even as overall critical-minerals investment fell 9%.

How to read these numbers

Included: IEA market value, demand, price, investment, and supply-balance figures for 2023–2025; the USGS final 2025 critical minerals list; IEA rare earth supply-chain shares for 2024; SMM and LME price readings from November 2026; and the U.S. Department of Energy and Department of Defense financing packages for Thacker Pass and MP Materials. Excluded: price forecasts presented as fact (Citigroup's $12,000 per tonne is a forecast, labeled as such); private market-research estimates of total market size that conflict with the IEA's published figure; and any blending of mining, refining, and magnet-production shares, which describe different stages of the chain.

Price readings are point-in-time assessments from the sources linked above and move daily; the “Last updated” stamp records when this page last verified them. Forecasts and planning targets are labeled as forecasts and targets, not actuals.
